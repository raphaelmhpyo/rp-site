Japanese Notes for Korean Speakers

Volume 1 — Weeks 1–2
An 8-week, conversation-first Japanese course built around Korean–Japanese similarities.

MISSION
By the end of Volume 1, you should be able to introduce yourself, ask and answer basic questions, describe people and things, use the core particles, tell simple present/past statements, order food, shop, talk about family, and construct your own basic Japanese sentences.

How to Use These Notes

Study for approximately 30–45 minutes per day. Every lesson follows the same sequence:

  1. Concept — one high-yield grammar idea.
  2. Korean bridge — exploit a similarity where it genuinely helps.
  3. Vocabulary — words you can immediately use.
  4. Pattern drills — substitute words rather than memorising isolated sentences.
  5. Conversation — use the grammar in a realistic exchange.
  6. Active recall — produce Japanese without looking.
IMPORTANT: Korean is a powerful shortcut, but not a perfect translation key. Whenever a Korean analogy is unsafe, this book explicitly labels the trap.

PART I — THE KOREAN–JAPANESE MAP

1. The Big Advantage

Japanese and Korean share several structural features that make Japanese unusually accessible to a Korean speaker:

ConceptJapaneseKorean
Basic orderS–O–VS–O–V
Topic markerは은/는
Subject markerが이/가
Object markerを을/를
Possessionの의
Alsoも도
Destinationに에
Location of actionで에서
Withと하고 / 와·과 / 랑·이랑
Fromから에서 / 부터
Untilまで까지
KOREAN SHORTCUT: When you encounter a new Japanese particle, first ask: “Does this behave like a Korean particle I already know?” If yes, use that similarity to form an initial mental model — then learn the Japanese-specific exceptions.

2. Sentence Architecture

私は日本語を勉強します。

Watashi wa nihongo o benkyō shimasu.

I study Japanese.

Literally:

私は = as for me
日本語を = Japanese + object marker
勉強します = study

저는 일본어를 공부해요.

The structural correspondence is striking:

私は ↔ 저는
日本語を ↔ 일본어를
勉強します ↔ 공부해요

CORE RULE: Japanese is usually much easier if you think in chunks: [topic] + [information about topic] + [verb/predicate], rather than translating every English word.

PART III — CORE GRAMMAR

LESSON 1 — です: “to be”

HIGH-YIELD
です is the polite sentence ending used with nouns and adjectives. It often corresponds broadly to Korean 이에요/예요.

私は医者です。

Watashi wa isha desu.

I am a doctor.

저는 의사예요.

JapaneseKoreanEnglish
学生です。학생이에요.I am a student.
日本人です。일본 사람이에요.I am Japanese.
オーストラリア人です。호주 사람이에요.I am Australian.
先生です。선생님이에요.I am a teacher.

Question: ～ですか？

医者ですか？

Are you a doctor?

はい、医者です。

Yes, I am a doctor.

いいえ、医者じゃないです。

No, I'm not a doctor.

KOREAN BRIDGE: ですか？ behaves similarly to 이에요?/예요? as a polite question ending.

Mini-drill

Say in Japanese:

  1. I am Australian.
  2. I am a doctor.
  3. I am not a student.
  4. Are you Japanese?
MEMORISE: はい = yes; いいえ = no; です = polite “is/am/are”; じゃないです = is/am/are not.

LESSON 2 — は and も

は = topic
Think approximately: Korean 은/는.

も = also
Think approximately: Korean 도.

私はラファエルです。

I am Raphael.

私はオーストラリア人です。

I am Australian.

妻もオーストラリア人です。

My wife is also Australian.

DON'T OVERTRANSLATE は. Japanese は marks the topic. It does not always map neatly onto an English “as for”. Korean speakers should initially use 은/는 as the mental anchor.

Contrast

私は医者です。妻も医者です。

I am a doctor. My wife is also a doctor.

Pattern

X は Y です。

X = topic; Y = description.

Conversation

A: お仕事は何ですか？
B: 医者です。
A: 奥さんは？
B: 医者です。

“What do you do for work?” → “I’m a doctor.” → “What about your wife?” → “She’s a doctor.”

IMPORTANT: Japanese frequently omits the subject when it is obvious. “奥さんは？” is perfectly natural; you do not need a full sentence.

LESSON 3 — が and basic existence

が marks the grammatical subject and is especially important with existence verbs, ability, likes/dislikes, and new information.

子どもがいます。

I have a child. / There is a child.

猫がいます。

There is a cat.

時間があります。

I have time. / There is time.

JapaneseKorean
子どもがいます。아이가 있어요.
お金があります。돈이 있어요.
時間があります。시간이 있어요.
KOREAN BRIDGE: Japanese が often behaves much like Korean 이/가. This is one of the easiest high-value transfers.

います vs あります

VerbUseExample
いますpeople/animals子どもがいます。
ありますthings/objects/abstract things車があります。

LESSON 5 — に: destination and time

に has several jobs. For now master two: destination and specific time.

Destination

日本に行きます。

I go to Japan.

京都に行きます。

I go to Kyoto.

🇰🇷 に ≈ 에 for destination: 일본에 가요 → 日本に行きます。

Specific time

7時に起きます。

I get up at 7.

10時に寝ます。

I go to bed at 10.

Important exception

Do not automatically add に to every time expression.

今日、行きます。

I’m going today.

明日、行きます。

I’m going tomorrow.

Natural Japanese commonly omits に with relative time words such as 今日, 明日, 昨日.

LESSON 6 — で: where an action happens

で marks the place where an action occurs. Think Korean 에서 in this use.

レストランで食べます。

I eat at a restaurant.

家で仕事をします。

I work at home.

ホテルで寝ます。

I sleep at the hotel.

JapaneseKorean
学校で勉強します。학교에서 공부해요.
東京で働いています。도쿄에서 일하고 있어요.
HIGH-YIELD DISTINCTION
に = destination / existence location
で = location of an action

東京に行きます。 = I go to Tokyo.
東京で食べます。 = I eat in Tokyo.

LESSON 7 — の: possession and description

の is broadly analogous to Korean 의, but Japanese uses it very frequently.

私の名前

my name

日本の食べ物

Japanese food

東京のホテル

a Tokyo hotel / hotel in Tokyo

妻の車

my wife's car

PATTERN: A の B = B associated with A. Context determines the exact English translation.

LESSON 8 — と: “and” / “with”

With

家族と行きます。

I go with my family.

友達と食べます。

I eat with a friend.

Quotation

「ありがとう」と言いました。

I said “thank you”.

Korean bridge

と can correspond to 하고 / 와·과 / 랑·이랑 depending on context.

LESSON 10 — Present and past with ます

For now, master the polite verb pattern:
～ます → ～ません → ～ました → ～ませんでした
PositiveNegative
Present/future行きます行きません
Past行きました行きませんでした

昨日、東京に行きました。

I went to Tokyo yesterday.

今日は行きません。

I’m not going today.

昨日、仕事をしませんでした。

I didn't work yesterday.

KOREAN BRIDGE: The politeness and tense system is conceptually familiar: 가요 / 안 가요 / 갔어요 / 안 갔어요 → 行きます / 行きません / 行きました / 行きませんでした.

PART VI — MINI CONVERSATION

A: はじめまして。お名前は何ですか？

B: ラファエルです。よろしくお願いします。

A: オーストラリア人ですか？

B: はい、オーストラリア人です。

A: どこに住んでいますか？

B: メルボルンに住んでいます。

A: お仕事は何ですか？

B: 医者です。

A: 日本語を勉強していますか？

B: はい、勉強しています。でも、まだ上手じゃないです。

Translation:

Nice to meet you. What's your name?
I'm Raphael. Nice to meet you.
Are you Australian?
Yes, I'm Australian.
Where do you live?
I live in Melbourne.
What do you do for work?
I'm a doctor.
Are you studying Japanese?
Yes, I am. But I'm not good yet.
