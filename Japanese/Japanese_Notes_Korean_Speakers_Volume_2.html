Japanese Notes for Korean Speakers

Volume 2 — Weeks 3–4
The Japanese Verb System + the Conversation Engine

MISSION
Volume 1 gave you the sentence skeleton and particles. Volume 2 gives you the machinery that makes Japanese sentences flexible: verb groups, ます / dictionary / ない / た / て forms, requests, ongoing actions, wants, permission, prohibition and basic ability.
WHY THIS VOLUME MATTERS FOR A KOREAN SPEAKER
Japanese verbs are highly regular once you recognise the patterns. More importantly, the functions are strikingly familiar:

食べたい ↔ 먹고 싶다
食べてください ↔ 먹어 주세요
食べてもいい ↔ 먹어도 되다
食べてはいけない ↔ 먹으면 안 되다
食べている ↔ 먹고 있다
食べられる ↔ 먹을 수 있다

Your task is therefore not to memorise isolated Japanese grammar points. It is to build a Japanese conjugation reflex.

How to Study Volume 2

  1. Learn the three verb groups.
  2. Master the five core forms: ます, dictionary, ない, た, て.
  3. Use those forms in sentences immediately.
  4. Do not move on until you can produce them aloud without thinking about every individual letter.
  5. Use Korean as a conceptual bridge, not as a word-for-word translation system.
THE BIG TRAP: Do not try to learn Japanese verb conjugation by translating English grammar terminology. Learn each Japanese form by its function + formation + Korean analogue + example.

PART I — THE JAPANESE VERB SYSTEM

1. Three Verb Groups

GroupTypical dictionary endingExampleMeaning
Group 1 / Godanusually an -u sound行くgo
Group 1 / Godanう, つ, る, む, ぶ, ぬ, く, ぐ, す飲むdrink
Group 2 / Ichidanmany verbs ending ～る after an i/e sound食べるeat
Group 3 / Irregularする / 来るするdo
HIGH-YIELD: Group 2 is wonderfully easy. Remove る and attach the new ending.

食べる → 食べます / 食べない / 食べた / 食べて
見る → 見ます / 見ない / 見た / 見て
CAUTION: Some ～る verbs are Group 1. The most useful early examples are 帰る (return), 入る (enter), 走る (run), 切る (cut), 知る (know). These must be learned individually.

Think of the dictionary form as the verb's “home address”. The other forms are tools you derive from it.

LESSON 2 — DICTIONARY FORM

The dictionary form is essential because many important grammar constructions attach to it.

日本に行く。

I go / will go to Japan. (plain style)

日本に行くと思います。

I think I'll go to Japan.

日本に行くつもりです。

I intend to go to Japan.

KOREAN CONNECTION: Japanese dictionary form behaves much like Korean dictionary form:

行く ↔ 가다
食べる ↔ 먹다
飲む ↔ 마시다
見る ↔ 보다
DON'T PANIC ABOUT PLAIN STYLE. Dictionary form does not mean “rude”. It is simply the plain form. You will encounter it constantly inside longer polite sentences.

LESSON 3 — ない FORM

Function: plain negative.

食べる → 食べない
行く → 行かない

Group 2

Remove る + ない.

食べる → 食べない
見る → 見ない
寝る → 寝ない

Group 1

Change the final u-row sound to the a-row sound + ない.

Dictionaryない
行く行かない
飲む飲まない
話す話さない
買う買わない
読む読まない
IMPORTANT EXCEPTION: する → しない; 来る（くる）→ 来ない（こない）.

Special: ある → ない.

Useful sentences

今日は行かないです。

I'm not going today. Common conversational polite-negative pattern.

お酒は飲まないです。

I don't drink alcohol.

안 가요 → 行かないです
안 먹어요 → 食べないです
안 마셔요 → 飲まないです

LESSON 5 — て FORM

THE MOST IMPORTANT FORM IN THIS VOLUME.
The て-form is not one single English tense. It is a connector used in many high-frequency constructions.

Group 2

見る → 見て
寝る → 寝て

Group 1

Endingて-formExample

む・ぶ・ぬ → んで

The closest mental model is often Korean -고 / -아(서) / -어 주세요 / -어도 / -고 있다, depending on the construction. There is no single one-to-one Korean translation for て.

LESSON 6 — ～てください: “please do...”

て-form + ください = please do X.
JapaneseMeaningKorean

聞いてください。Please listen.들어 주세요.
待ってください。Please wait.기다려 주세요.

Essential phrase

もう一度言ってください。

Please say it one more time.

ゆっくり話してください。

Please speak slowly.

THIS IS A CONVERSATION SUPERPOWER: if you don't understand, don't freeze. Ask for repetition or slower speech.

LESSON 7 — ～ています: “am/is doing”

て-form + います commonly describes an ongoing action or a current state.

日本語を勉強しています。

I am studying Japanese.

東京に住んでいます。

I live in Tokyo.

今、食べています。

I'm eating now.

仕事をしています。

I'm working.

KOREAN BRIDGE:
공부하고 있어요 → 勉強しています
먹고 있어요 → 食べています
일하고 있어요 → 仕事をしています
IMPORTANT: ～ています is not always literally “currently doing”. With certain verbs it expresses a continuing state:

結婚しています = I am married.
住んでいます = I live/reside.
知っています = I know.

LESSON 8 — ～たい: “want to...”

ます-stem + たい
This is one of the highest-yield constructions in conversational Japanese.
ますWant toKorean
行きます行きたい가고 싶다
食べます食べたい먹고 싶다
飲みます飲みたい마시고 싶다
見ます見たい보고 싶다
買います買いたい사고 싶다
しますしたい하고 싶다

京都に行きたいです。

I want to go to Kyoto.

ラーメンを食べたいです。

I want to eat ramen.

何をしたいですか？

What do you want to do?

Negative

行きたくないです。

I don't want to go.

Near-perfect conceptual transfer: 가고 싶어요 → 行きたいです; 먹고 싶어요 → 食べたいです.

LESSON 9 — ～てもいいです: permission

て-form + もいいです = it is okay to do X / may I do X?

ここに座ってもいいですか？

May I sit here?

写真を撮ってもいいですか？

May I take a photo?

入ってもいいですか？

May I come in?

먹어도 돼요? → 食べてもいいですか？
가도 돼요? → 行ってもいいですか？

Answer

はい、いいです。

Yes, that's fine.

すみません、ちょっと……。

Sorry, perhaps not…

LESSON 10 — ～てはいけません: prohibition

て-form + はいけません = must not / may not.

ここで写真を撮ってはいけません。

You must not take photos here.

ここに入ってはいけません。

You must not enter here.

먹으면 안 돼요 → 食べてはいけません
들어가면 안 돼요 → 入ってはいけません
SPOKEN JAPANESE: In everyday conversation you will often hear shorter alternatives such as ～ちゃだめ / ～じゃだめ. Learn to recognise them before worrying about producing them.

PART III — THE CONVERSATION ENGINE

LESSON 13 — Combining clauses with て

The て-form can connect actions in sequence.

朝起きて、コーヒーを飲みます。

I get up in the morning and drink coffee.

ホテルに行って、チェックインします。

I go to the hotel and check in.

買い物をして、ホテルに帰ります。

I shop and then return to the hotel.

일어나서 커피를 마셔요 → 朝起きて、コーヒーを飲みます.

LESSON 14 — から: because

Sentence + から gives a reason: because...

疲れたから、帰ります。

I'm tired, so I'm going home.

忙しいから、行きません。

I'm busy, so I won't go.

おいしいから、好きです。

I like it because it's delicious.

피곤하니까 집에 가요 → 疲れたから、帰ります.

LESSON 15 — ～ほうがいい: should

Dictionary form + ほうがいい = it is better to do X / you should do X.

ない form + ほうがいい = it is better not to do X.

早く寝たほうがいいです。

You should go to bed early.

もっと勉強したほうがいいです。

You should study more.

無理しないほうがいいです。

You shouldn't push yourself.

하는 게 좋아요 → ～たほうがいいです
하지 않는 게 좋아요 → ～ないほうがいいです

LESSON 16 — ～つもり: intention

Dictionary form + つもりです = intend/plan to do.
ない form + つもりです = intend not to do.

来年、日本に行くつもりです。

I intend to go to Japan next year.

今日は外食するつもりです。

I intend to eat out today.

明日は仕事をしないつもりです。

I intend not to work tomorrow.

할 생각이에요 → ～つもりです

PART V — KOREAN TRANSFER: USE IT / DON'T TRUST IT

USE IT

KoreanJapaneseConfidence
먹고 싶어요食べたいですVery high
먹어 주세요食べてくださいVery high
먹어도 돼요?食べてもいいですか？Very high
먹으면 안 돼요食べてはいけませんHigh

먹을 생각이에요食べるつもりですHigh

DON'T TRUST IT BLINDLY

1. ～고 싶다 ≠ “miss” in every context.
Korean 보고 싶다 can mean “I miss [someone]”. Japanese 会いたい means “want to meet/see someone”; “miss someone” has to be expressed contextually.
2. Japanese いる/ある ≠ one universal Korean 있다 rule.
Japanese distinguishes animate いる and inanimate ある.
3. Japanese は/が is not a perfect 은/는/이/가 substitution.
The overlap is huge, but Japanese information structure and contrast patterns require separate learning.
4. Politeness is similar but not identical.
です/ます, だ, casual endings and honorific forms do not map one-to-one onto Korean speech levels.
