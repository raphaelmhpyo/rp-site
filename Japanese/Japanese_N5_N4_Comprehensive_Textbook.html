1. The Japanese Writing Systems Overview

Learning Objective: Master how Hiragana, Katakana, and Kanji function together dynamically in written Japanese sentences.

Unlike English, which relies on a single 26-letter alphabet, written Japanese uses three interconnected scripts simultaneously. Understanding when and why each script is used is step zero of learning Japanese.

1. Hiragana (ひらがな)

Hiragana is the foundational phonetic alphabet consisting of 46 primary characters. It represents every native phonetic sound in the language. It is used for:

  • Grammatical particles (e.g., は, が, を)
  • Verb and adjective inflectional endings (okurigana)
  • Native words that lack Kanji or whose Kanji is overly obscure

2. Katakana (カタカナ)

Katakana is angular and structured, directly mirroring the 46 sounds of Hiragana. It is utilized for:

  • Foreign loanwords (gairaigo) e.g., コーヒー (Coffee)
  • Foreign personal and country names e.g., オーストラリア (Australia)
  • Onomatopoeia and sound effects
  • Scientific terms, plant/animal names, and textual emphasis (similar to italics)

3. Kanji (漢字)

Ideographic characters originating from China. Each character conveys an intrinsic meaning or core concept. Kanji provides structural visual boundaries in Japanese sentences, which do not traditionally use spaces.

Anatomy of a Sentence:
私 は コーヒー を 飲みたい です。
• 私 (Kanji: I/Me)
• は (Hiragana: Topic Particle)
• コーヒー (Katakana: Foreign Word "Coffee")
• を (Hiragana: Object Particle)
• 飲み (Kanji + Hiragana: Verb stem "Drink")
• たい です (Hiragana: Auxiliary ending "Want to" + Copula)

Special Katakana Extension Rules

  • Long Vowels (ー): A horizontal bar extends vowel sounds by 1 mora. e.g., ケーキ (Keeki = Cake), タクシー (Takushii = Taxi).

    • ファ (fa), フィ (fi), フェ (fe), フォ (fo)
    • ティ (ti), ディ (di)
    • ウィ (wi), ウェ (we)

4. Kanji Fundamentals: Onyomi, Kunyomi & Radicals

Learning Objective: Demystify Kanji readings and learn how character components (radicals) build complex meanings.

1. On'yomi (音読み) vs. Kun'yomi (訓読み)

Most Kanji possess at least two distinct reading categories:

TypeOriginWhen UsedExample (水 - Water)
Kun'yomi (Japanese Reading) Native Japanese words When the Kanji stands alone as an independent word みず (mizu) e.g., 水を飲む
On'yomi (Chinese Reading) Borrowed Chinese sound When joined with other Kanji to form compound words (Jukugo) スイ (sui) e.g., 水曜日 (Sui'youbi)

2. Radicals (部首 - Bushu)

Kanji are constructed from building blocks called Radicals. Recognizing radicals allows you to infer meaning and pronunciation:

  • 氵 (Sanzui - Water): Appears in words related to liquids. e.g., 泳 (Swim), 海 (Ocean), 洗 (Wash).
  • 亻 (Ninben - Person): Appears in words relating to human activity. e.g., 休 (Rest), 体 (Body), 作 (Make).
  • 木 (Ki - Tree/Wood): Appears in plant/wood items. e.g., 林 (Forest), 森 (Woods), 本 (Book/Origin).

5. Pitch Accent & Pronunciation Foundations

Learning Objective: Master Pitch Accent dynamics (Heiban, Atamadaka, Nakadaka, Odaka) to achieve natural spoken rhythm.

English uses Stress Accent (louder/longer syllables). Japanese uses Pitch Accent (high vs. low tone per mora).

The 4 Pitch Accent Patterns

  1. 平板 (Heiban - Flat): Low initial mora, rises to High, stays High throughout particles. (e.g., 水 - mizu).
  2. 頭高 (Atamadaka - Head-High): First mora is High, immediately drops to Low for remaining morae and particles. (e.g., 雨 - ame [Rain]).
  3. 中高 (Nakadaka - Mid-High): Starts Low, rises to High inside the word, drops to Low before the end.
  4. 尾高 (Odaka - Tail-High): Starts Low, rises to High on final mora, drops to Low only when attached particle follows. (e.g., 飴 - ame [Candy]).

6. Sentence Structure (SOV) & Core Word Order

Learning Objective: Build structured Japanese sentences using Subject-Object-Verb alignment.

English follows Subject-Verb-Object (SVO). Japanese strictly enforces Subject-Object-Verb (SOV). The primary verb ALWAYS closes the sentence.

Japanese Sentence Core:
[Topic / Subject] は/が + [Time / Place] に/で + [Object] を + [VERB]
田中さんは明日図書室で本を読みます。
Tanaka-san wa ashita toshoshitsu de hon o yomimasu.
Tanaka-san will read a book in the library tomorrow.
• 田中さん (Subject) + は (Topic)
• 明日 (Time: Tomorrow)
• 図書室 (Location) + で (Action Location Particle)
• 本 (Object: Book) + を (Object Particle)
• 読みます (Verb: Read)

7. Topic Particle (は) vs Subject Particle (が)

Learning Objective: Differentiate between Topic marker は (wa) and Subject marker が (ga) in real contexts.

1. は (wa) - The Topic Marker

Think of は as: "As for X, [statement]...". It sets the overarching context. What comes AFTER は contains the new, important information.

私 は 医者 です。
Watashi wa isha desu.
As for me, I am a doctor. (Focus is on being a doctor)

2. が (ga) - The Subject Marker

が identifies a specific subject doing an action or possessing a state. The emphasis is on WHAT/WHO comes BEFORE が.

誰 が 来ましたか。 田中さん が 来ました。
Dare ga kimashita ka. Tanaka-san ga kimashita.
Who came? Tanaka-san came. (Focus is pinpointing Tanaka-san)

Key Contrast Matrix

FeatureParticle は (wa)Particle が (ga)
Primary FocusInformation following the particleThe subject noun preceding the particle
Question WordsCannot directly follow question words (*誰は ❌)Required after question words (誰が ⭕)
ContrastUsed to contrast two things (Aは...Bは...)Exhibits exclusive selection ("X and X alone")

9. Verb Group Classification (Group 1, Group 2, Group 3)

Learning Objective: Correctly identify Godan, Ichidan, and Irregular verbs using dictionary forms.

Every conjugation rule in Japanese relies on correctly placing a verb into one of three groups:

Group 1: Godan Verbs (五段動詞 / U-Verbs)

Dictionary forms end in: ~う (u), ~く (ku), ~ぐ (gu), ~す (su), ~つ (tsu), ~ぬ (nu), ~ぶ (bu), ~む (mu), or ~る (ru) (preceded by a, u, or o sounds).

  • 飲む (nomu - to drink)
  • 行く (iku - to go)
  • 話す (hanasu - to speak)

Group 2: Ichidan Verbs (一段動詞 / Ru-Verbs)

Dictionary forms end in ~る (ru), preceded by an 'i' sound (e.g., ~iru) or an 'e' sound (e.g., ~eru).

  • 食べる (taberu - to eat)
  • 見る (miru - to see)

⚠️ Critical Exceptions (Look like Group 2, but are Group 1 Godan): 帰る (kaeru - return), 入る (hairu - enter), 走る (hashiru - run), 起きる (okiru - wake up: Group 2), 切る (kiru - cut: Group 1).

Group 3: Irregular Verbs (不規則動詞)

Contains only two primary verbs:

  • する (suru - to do)
  • 来る (kuru - to come)

10. Polite Present/Future Form (~ます)

Learning Objective: Conjugate dictionary verbs into polite present and past statements.

Conjugation Rules

  • Group 1 (Godan): Shift final ~u sound to ~i sound line, add ます.
    e.g., 書く (kaku) → 書きます (kakimasu) | 飲む (nomu) → 飲みます (nomimasu).
  • Group 2 (Ichidan): Drop ~る, add ます.
    e.g., 食べる (taberu) → 食べます (tabemasu).
  • Group 3 (Irregular):
    • する → します (shimasu)
    • 来る (kuru) → 来ます (kimasu)
FormEndingExample (書く - Write)Example (食べる - Eat)
Present Affirmative~ます書きます食べます
Present Negative~ません書きません食べません
Past Affirmative~ました書きました食べました
Past Negative~ませんでした書きませんでした食べませんでした

15. The Master Te-Form (て形) Transformation Mechanics

Learning Objective: Master the Te-form conjugation across all verb classes.

The Te-form is the single most essential inflection in Japanese grammar. It acts as the anchor for connecting clauses, making requests, stating progress, and forming permissions.

Group 1 (Godan) Te-Form Rules

Look at the final dictionary syllable of the Godan verb:

Ending SyllablesTe-Form ChangeExample DictionaryTe-Form Result
う, つ, る~って (tte)買う (kau) / 待つ (matsu)買って / 待って
む, ぶ, ぬ~んで (nde)飲む (nomu) / 遊ぶ (asobu)飲んで / 遊んで
く~いて (ite)書く (kaku)書いて
ぐ~いで (ide)泳ぐ (oyogu)泳いで
す~して (shite)話す (hanasu)話して

⚠️ Essential Exception: 行く (iku - to go) becomes 行って (itte) [NOT いいて].

Group 2 & 3 Te-Form Rules

  • Group 2 (Ichidan): Drop ~る, add て. e.g., 食べる → 食べて | 見る → 見て.
  • Group 3 (Irregular):
    • する → して
    • 来る (kuru) → 来て (kite)

17. Ongoing Actions & Resultant States (~ている)

Learning Objective: Express continuous action (is doing) versus resultant state (is currently in a state of).
Formula: [Verb Te-form] + いる / います

1. Continuous Action (-ing)

Action verbs express ongoing activity happening right now:

今、朝ごはんを食べています。
Ima, asagohan o tabete imasu.
I am eating breakfast right now.

2. Resultant State

Change-of-state verbs express a past action whose state continues into the present:

結婚しています / 知っています / 東京に住んでいます。
Kekkon shite imasu / Shitte imasu / Toukyou ni sunde imasu.
I am married / I know / I live in Tokyo.

23. Potential Form: Ability to Do (~できる / ~れる)

Learning Objective: Conjugate verbs into potential form to express capability ("can do").

Conjugation System

  • Group 1 (Godan): Change final ~u vowel to the ~e vowel line, add る.
    e.g., 書く (kaku) → 書ける (kakeru) | 話す (hanasu) → 話せる (hanaseru).
  • Group 2 (Ichidan): Drop ~る, add られる (Casual spoken: れる).
    e.g., 食べる → 食べられる (taberareru).
  • Group 3 (Irregular):
    • する → できる (dekiru)
    • 来る (kuru) → 来られる (korareru)

⚠️ Particle Rule Change: Direct object particle を switches to が when using potential form!

私は日本語が話せます。
Watashi wa Nihongo ga hanasemasu.
I can speak Japanese.

30. Passive (~られる) & Causative Voice (~させる)

Learning Objective: Master passive voice ("action done to subject") and causative voice ("make/let someone do").

1. Passive Voice (受身形)

Used when the subject receives or suffers an action performed by an agent (marked by に):

  • Group 1: ~u sound → ~a sound + れる (褒める → 褒められる / 書く → 書かれる)
  • Group 2: Drop ~る + られる (食べる → 食べられる)
  • Group 3: する → される | 来る → 来られる
私は先生に褒められました。
Watashi wa sensei ni homeramashita.
I was praised by my teacher.

2. Causative Voice (使役形)

Used to express making someone do something, or allowing/letting someone do something:

  • Group 1: ~u sound → ~a sound + させる (行く → 行かせる)
  • Group 2: Drop ~る + させる (食べる → 食べさせる)
  • Group 3: する → させる | 来る → 来させる
お母さんは子供に野菜を食べさせました。
Okaasan wa kodomo ni yasai o tabesasemashita.
The mother made her child eat vegetables.
