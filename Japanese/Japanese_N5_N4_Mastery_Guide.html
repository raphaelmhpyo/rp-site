Module 1: Foundations & Writing Systems

1. The Three Japanese Writing Systems

N5 ⏱️ 5 min
Goal: Understand how Hiragana, Katakana, and Kanji work together in Japanese sentences.
Japanese uses three scripts simultaneously:
  • Hiragana (ひらがな): Phonetic script (46 basic characters) used for native words, particles, and verb endings.
  • Katakana (カタカナ): Phonetic script (46 basic characters) used primarily for loanwords, foreign names, and emphasis.
  • Kanji (漢字): Ideographic characters borrowed from Chinese, representing core words, concepts, and roots.
Example Breakdown: 私 は パン を 食べます。 (Watashi wa pan o tabemasu.)
• 私 (Kanji = I) • は (Hiragana particle) • パン (Katakana = Bread) • を (Hiragana particle) • 食べます (Kanji + Hiragana verb)
アメリカ人です。
Amerika-jin desu.
I am American. (アメリカ = Katakana; 人 = Kanji; です = Hiragana)
Quick Check

Which writing system is used for foreign loanwords like "coffee" (コーヒー)?

Show Answer

Katakana! Foreign loanwords are written in Katakana.

2. Pronunciation & Pitch Accent Basics

N5 ⏱️ 5 min
Goal: Master the 5 vowel sounds and understand Pitch Accent.
Japanese pronunciation is remarkably consistent. There are only 5 basic vowels: A (あ), I (い), U (う), E (え), O (お). Unlike English stress accent, Japanese uses Pitch Accent (high vs. low pitch).
Vowels: あ (ah as in 'father'), い (ee as in 'feet'), う (oo as in 'boot'), え (eh as in 'bed'), お (oh as in 'go').
雨 (あめ - High to Low) vs 飴 (あめ - Low to High)
Ame (Rain) vs Ame (Candy)
Pitch changes the meaning of homophones!

3. SOV Sentence Structure & Word Order

N5 ⏱️ 7 min
Goal: Understand why verbs always go at the very end of Japanese sentences.
English is Subject-Verb-Object (SVO: "I eat apples"). Japanese is Subject-Object-Verb (SOV). The verb ALWAYS comes last!
English Order: [Subject] [Verb] [Object]
Japanese Order: [Subject + Topic Particle] [Object + Particle] [VERB]
田中さんはりんごを食べます。
Tanaka-san wa ringo o tabemasu.
Tanaka eats an apple. (Lit: Tanaka [topic] apple [object] eats)
Quick Check

Where does the verb go in a Japanese sentence?

Show Answer

At the very end of the sentence.

4. Topic (は) vs Subject (が)

N5 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Demystify the fundamental difference between particles は (wa) and が (ga).
• は (wa): Marks the TOPIC ("As for X..."). It provides context or sets contrast.
• が (ga): Marks the SPECIFIC SUBJECT. It introduces new information or emphasizes WHO/WHAT is doing the action.
• [X] は ... = "Speaking of [X]..." (Focus is on what follows)
• [X] が ... = "[X] is the one who..." (Focus is on [X] itself)
私 は 学生です。
Watashi wa gakusei desu.
As for me, I am a student.
誰 が 来ましたか。田中さん が 来ました。
Dare ga kimashita ka. Tanaka-san ga kimashita.
Who came? Tanaka-san came (Tanaka is the specific answer).

Module 2: Essential Japanese Grammar & N5 Core

6. The Copula: です (Desu) & Negation

N5 ⏱️ 6 min
Goal: Express "A is B" and "A is not B" in polite present tense.
です (desu) acts like "is/am/are". To make it negative, replace です with ではありません (de wa arimasen) or casual じゃないです (ja nai desu).
• Affirmative: [N1] は [N2] です。
• Negative: [N1] は [N2] ではありません / じゃないです。
• Past: [N1] は [N2] でした。
私は先生ではありません。
Watashi wa sensei de wa arimasen.
I am not a teacher.

7. Verb Conjugation Groups (Group 1, 2, 3)

N5 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Categorize Japanese verbs into 3 groups to master all future conjugations.
Every Japanese verb belongs to one of three groups:
  • Group 1 (Godan / U-verbs): End in ~u, ~ku, ~gu, ~su, ~tsu, ~nu, ~bu, ~mu, or ~ru (preceded by a/o/u). e.g., 飲む (nomu), 行く (iku).
  • Group 2 (Ichidan / Ru-verbs): End in ~ru (preceded by 'i' or 'e' sound). e.g., 食べる (taberu), 見る (miru).
  • Group 3 (Irregular): Only two main verbs: する (suru - to do) and 来る (kuru - to come).

8. Polite Present & Future Tense (〜ます)

N5 ⏱️ 7 min
Goal: Conjugate verbs into polite present/future form (~masu) and past form (~mashita).
• Group 1: Shift root vowel ~u to ~i + ます (行く → 行きます)
• Group 2: Drop ~る + ます (表べる → 食べます)
• Group 3: する → します | 来る → 来ます (きます)
明日、東京へ行きます。
Ashita, Toukyou e ikimasu.
I will go to Tokyo tomorrow.
昨日、映画を見ました。
Kinou, eiga o mimashita.
I watched a movie yesterday.

9. Direct Object Particle: を (o)

N5 ⏱️ 5 min
Goal: Mark the noun receiving the action of an active verb.
The particle を (pronounced "o") marks the direct object of an action verb.
[Noun] を [Action Verb]
水を飲みます。
Mizu o nomimasu.
I drink water.

10. Destination & Time Particles: に (ni) & へ (e)

N5 ⏱️ 6 min
Goal: Correctly express destinations and specific time points.
• に (ni) marks specific points in time (7時に - at 7 o'clock) AND destinations.
• へ (e) marks direction/movement towards a destination ("heading towards").
７時に学校へ行きます。
Shichi-ji ni gakkou e ikimasu.
I go to school at 7 o'clock.

11. Location of Action Particle: で (de)

N5 ⏱️ 5 min
Goal: Differentiate location of action (で) from destination (に).
When an action takes place AT a location, mark the location with で. It also means "by means of" (e.g. by train).
図書館で勉強します。
Toshokan de benkyou shimasu.
I study AT the library.
電車で行きます。
Densha de ikimasu.
I will go BY train.

12. Adjectives: い-Adjectives vs な-Adjectives

N5 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Learn how to conjugate and attach both types of adjectives to nouns.
Japanese adjectives function like mini-verbs and fall into two classes:
  • い-adjectives (i-adj): End in ~い. Conjugate directly (e.g. 大きい → 大きくない).
  • な-adjectives (na-adj): Require な when modifying nouns directly (e.g. 静かな町 - quiet town). Conjugate using です/ではありません.
• i-adj negative: Drop ~い + くない (たかい → たかくない)
• na-adj + noun: [Adj] な [Noun] (きれいな人)

13. Master the Te-Form (て形)

N5 ⏱️ 10 min
Goal: Learn the single most versatile verb transformation in Japanese.
The Te-form is used to connect sentences, make requests, express ongoing state, and grant permission.
Group 1 Rules:
• う, つ, る → った / って (買っていく → 買って)
• む, ぶ, ぬ → んだ / んで (飲む → 飲んで)
• く → いた / いて (書く → 書いて) [Exception: 行く → 行って]
• ぐ → いだ / いで (泳ぐ → 泳いで)
• す → した / して (話す → 話して)
Group 2: Drop ~る + て (食べる → 食べて)
Group 3: する → して | 来る → 来て(きて)

16. Casual / Short Form (Plain Form)

N4 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Use dictionary form, ta-form, nai-form, and plain copula with friends and family.
JLPT N4 grammar relies heavily on combining the Casual/Short Form with various grammar structures.
• Present Affirmative: Dictionary Form (食べる)
• Present Negative: Nai Form (食べない)
• Past Affirmative: Ta Form (食べた)
• Past Negative: Nakatta Form (食べなかった)

17. Quotations & Thoughts: 〜と思う / 〜と言う

N4 ⏱️ 7 min
Goal: Express thoughts ("I think that...") and report statements ("They said that...").
[Plain Form Sentence] + と 思います (to omowimasu) = I think that...
[Plain Form Sentence] + と 言っていました (to itte imashita) = They said that...

Ashita, ame ga kuru to omoimasu.

18. Potential Form: Ability to Do (〜できる / 〜れる)

N4 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Conjugate verbs to express "can do X".
• Group 1: Shift ~u ending to ~e sound + る (話す → 話せる)
• Group 2: Drop ~る + られる (or casual れる) (食べる → 食べられる)
• Group 3: する → できる | 来る → 来られる(こられる)
Note: Direct object particle を usually changes to が with potential verbs!
日本語が話せます。
Nihongo ga hanasemasu.
I can speak Japanese.

19. Giving & Receiving: あげる, もらう, くれる

N4 ⏱️ 9 min
Goal: Master the direction of giving and receiving favors and physical items.
• あげる (ageru): I give to you / someone gives to another.
• くれる (kureru): Someone gives to ME (or my in-group).
• もらう (morau): I receive from someone.
田中さんが私に本をくれました。
Tanaka-san ga watashi ni hon o kuremashita.
Tanaka-san gave me a book.
友だちにプレゼントをあげました。
Tomodachi ni purezento o agemashita.
I gave a present to my friend.

20. Modifying Nouns with Verb Clauses

N4 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Create complex sentences like "The man who bought a car" without needing relative pronouns like 'who' or 'which'.
In Japanese, simply place a plain verb phrase directly BEFORE a noun to modify it.
[Plain Verb / Sentence] + [Noun]
昨日買った本
Kinou kat-ta hon
The book that I bought yesterday. (Lit: Yesterday-bought book)
あそこに立っている人は田中さんです。
Asoko ni tat-te iru hito wa Tanaka-san desu.
The person who is standing over there is Tanaka-san.

21. Expressing Desire: 〜たい (Want to do) & 〜ほしい (Want an object)

N4 ⏱️ 6 min
Goal: Express what you want to do (verb) vs what item you want (noun).
• Want to do (Verb stem + たい): 寿司が/を 食べたいです。(I want to eat sushi)
• Want an object (Noun + が ほしい): 新しい車が ほしいです。(I want a new car)

N4 ⏱️ 7 min
Goal: Form the most common conditional structure for "If [X] happens, then [Y]".

25. Hypothetical Conditional: 〜ば (If)

N4 ⏱️ 7 min
Goal: Form the general/hypothetical "if" condition.
• Verbs: Change ~u to ~eba (行く → 行けば)
• い-adj: Drop ~い + ければ (安ければ - if it is cheap)
• Noun / な-adj: Add なら(ば)
安ければ、買います。
Yasukereba, kaimasu.
If it is cheap, I will buy it.

26. Passive Voice: 〜られる / 〜れる

N4 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Express passive actions ("was eaten", "was spoken to", suffering passive).
• Group 1: Change ~u to ~a sound + れる (褒める/書く → 書かれる)
• Group 2: Drop ~る + られる (食べる → 食べられる)
• Group 3: する → される | 来る → 来られる(こられる)
先生に褒められました。
Sensei ni homeramashita.
I was praised by the teacher. (Agent of action marked by に)

27. Causative Voice: 〜させる (Make / Let someone do)

N4 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Express making or allowing someone to do an action.
• Group 1: Change ~u to ~a sound + させる (行く → 行かせる)
• Group 2: Drop ~る + させる (食べる → 食べさせる)
• Group 3: する → させる | 来る → 来させる(こさせる)
子供に野菜を食べさせます。
Kodomo ni yasai o tabesasemasu.
I make/let my child eat vegetables.

28. Introduction to Honorifics: Keigo (敬語)

N4 ⏱️ 8 min
Goal: Distinguish Honorific (尊敬語 Sonkeigo) and Humble (謙譲語 Kenjougo) speech.
• Sonkeigo (Respectful): Elevates the actions of the listener or customer.
• Kenjougo (Humble): Lowers your own actions to show respect to the listener.
